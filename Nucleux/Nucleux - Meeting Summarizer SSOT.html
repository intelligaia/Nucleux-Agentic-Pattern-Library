Framing — Prism is one app with many cooperating agents

Prism is a whole package of one app — an intelligent meeting summarizer and insight generator — in which multiple specialized agents work in combination behind a single product surface. It is not a library of separate products; it is one application whose pipeline is decomposed into nine agents that each own a slice of the work. Prism captures meetings (record audio, capture screen/video, upload a file, paste a Drive/Zoom/Meet link, or import from Google Calendar), produces summaries, decisions, and action items, generates insights, and — crucially — tracks how meeting insights and features evolve over time, detecting drift, and exporting finalized, actionable user stories to Jira and Git with two-way status sync. The nine agents working in combination:

› Orchestrator — routes work across the other agents and owns job state.
› Capture — records audio & screen/video, ingests uploads, links, and calendar events.
› Transcription — speech-to-text with speaker diarization.
› Summarizer — structured summary: context, key points, topics, keywords.
› Insight — generates cross-meeting insights, patterns, and recommendations.
› Action-item — extracts decisions and tasks with owners and due hints.
› Retrieval / Chat — conversational ask across the whole meeting corpus, cited.
› Tracking / Drift — follows a feature's lifecycle and flags when it goes off-track.
› Integration — exports user stories to Jira/Git with two-way status sync.

01 · Product vision

What Prism is

Prism is one app: an intelligent meeting summarizer and insight generator. It captures meetings from any source, transcribes them, and produces structured, actionable output — summaries, keywords, decisions, and tasks — then generates insights and keeps every recording in a shareable library. Above all, Prism tracks how meeting insights and features evolve over time: it follows a feature from inception to execution, detects drift (is this feature going the right way or off-track?), and exports finalized, actionable user stories to Jira and Git with two-way status sync — closing the loop from talk to shipped work.

Its capture surface is deliberately broad: record audio, capture screen/video (Loom-style), upload an existing file, paste a Google Drive / Zoom / Meet link, or import meetings from Google Calendar. Its intelligence surface borrows from Otter — live transcript, speaker identification, AI summary, keywords/topics, action items, powerful search, and "ask your meetings" — and from Loom — a recording library of video cards, async shareable playback, view tracking, and timestamped comments/reactions. The result is one place to capture and act on a meeting, share and revisit it, and watch the features it spawned evolve all the way to delivery.

Several capture paths sit at the front. The live path records audio and screen/video directly, or connects to a meeting platform — Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams, WebEx — via a bot attendee with participant awareness, gated behind a one-time onboarding consent. The ingest path accepts existing material: audio/video from the desktop, from Google Drive, from a shared Zoom/Meet link, or pulled in from a connected Google Calendar. The upload path is where Prism starts as a POC, because it delivers the core value — transcription, actionable notes, and first insights — without the integration and consent complexity of live capture.

Behind every path runs a multi-agent core inside the one app: nine cooperating agents — Orchestrator, Capture, Transcription, Summarizer, Insight, Action-item, Retrieval/Chat, Tracking/Drift, and Integration — that each own a slice of the pipeline. This is the architectural bet that separates Prism from a single-prompt tool: work is decomposed, specialized, and observable, so the app can scale from "summarize one upload" to "track a feature from a discovery call through to a closed Jira story, flagging drift along the way" without a rewrite. Prism is a whole package of one app with multiple agents working in combination — not a library of separate products.

02 · Product principles

Four commitments that govern every decision

01

Agentic-first

Every capability is owned by a named agent with clear inputs and outputs, all inside the one app. We compose agents, not megaprompts.

02

Capture anywhere

Live or uploaded, Meet or Zoom or a file on disk — the source should never limit the output.

03

Actionable to execution

A summary is the floor. Decisions, owners, insights, and shipped stories tracked to a closed Jira/Git status are the real deliverable.

04

Consent-first

Recording is opt-in and transparent. One-time, explicit consent governs all meeting data use.

03 · Users & primary use cases

Who it's for

  • Project / delivery leads — need a reliable record of decisions and a way to see whether a project is progressing or going in circles.
  • Product & design teams — turn discovery calls, reviews, and client meetings into notes and tasks without manual write-ups.
  • Individual contributors — catch up on a missed meeting in two minutes; find "what did we decide about X" across weeks of calls.
  • Client-facing teams — produce clean, shareable summaries and follow-ups straight after a call.

Core jobs to be done

  • Transcribe a meeting or an uploaded recording accurately, with speakers attributed.
  • Get a structured summary: context, key points, decisions, and action items with owners.
  • Generate insights — patterns, risks, and recommendations across meetings.
  • Track how a feature evolves from inception to execution and be warned when it drifts off-track.
  • Turn discussion into finalized user stories and push them to Jira / Git with status sync.
  • Ask questions across past meetings and get cited answers; see what's repeating, what changed, what shipped.

06 · The agentic layer

The nine agents that run Prism

Prism is one app, and its pipeline is decomposed into nine specialized agents working in combination, coordinated by an Orchestrator. Each agent has a clear trigger, a single responsibility, and typed inputs/outputs — so we can build, test, swap, and scale them independently. Agents marked In POC are part of the Phase 1 build. These are not separate products; they are cooperating agents inside the single Prism application, spanning capture all the way through to feature/drift tracking and Jira/Git execution sync.

O

Orchestrator agent In POC

Coordinator · routes work, tracks state

Owns the lifecycle of a meeting job. Decides which agents run, in what order, retries failures, and exposes job status. The single observable control plane.

In meeting job (source, metadata)  ·  Out ordered agent invocations, job state

C

Capture agent In POC live · Phase 2

Records & ingests from every source

The front door. Records neural audio and screen/video in-app, accepts uploads and Drive/Zoom/Meet links, imports from Google Calendar, and (Phase 2) joins Meet/Zoom/Teams/WebEx as a visible bot attendee — streaming chunks to storage with consent awareness.

In recording / file / link / calendar event, consent token  ·  Out media artifact, source metadata

T

Transcription agent In POC

Speech-to-text · diarization

Converts audio (uploaded or live) to a timestamped, speaker-attributed transcript. Normalizes across sources and languages.

In audio artifact  ·  Out structured transcript (segments, speakers, timestamps)

M

Summarizer agent In POC

Transcript → structured summary

Produces the summary card: context, key points, topics. Tuned prompts and a stable output schema so downstream agents and the UI can rely on it.

In transcript  ·  Out summary object (context, key points, topics)

A

Action-item agent In POC

Extracts decisions, tasks, owners

The "actionable" engine. Pulls explicit decisions and action items, attributes owners, infers due hints, and formats for export or downstream push.

In transcript + summary  ·  Out decisions[], action items[] (owner, due, source span)

I

Insight agent Phase 2

Generates insights across meetings

The insight-generation engine. Aggregates across a project's meetings to surface recurring topics, emerging patterns, risks, and recommendations — feeding insight cards, the weekly digest, and the feedback→insight→story pipeline.

In all project meetings, decisions, action items  ·  Out insights, topic trends, recommendations

R

Retrieval / chat agent Phase 2

RAG over the meeting corpus

Powers "ask your meetings." Embeds transcripts and summaries into a vector store and answers questions conversationally with citations back to the source segment.

In user query, corpus  ·  Out cited, streamed answer

D

Tracking / drift agent Phase 3

Follows feature evolution · flags drift

Tracks how a feature evolves across the meetings that shape it — inception to execution — and scores deviation from original intent. Detects whether a feature is going the right way or off-track, raises a drift/deviation meter, and flags changed decisions for resolution.

In feature timeline, decisions, insights  ·  Out lifecycle state, drift score, deviation alerts

N

Integration agent Phase 3

Jira / Git export & two-way sync

Closes the loop to execution. Exports finalized, actionable user stories to Jira and Git, maps owners and fields, and syncs status back so Prism reflects shipped/closed state against the source story.

In finalized stories, action items  ·  Out Jira issues, Git branches/PRs, synced status

08 · Architecture & consent

System shape

A frontend (React/Next.js) over an API layer that hands meeting jobs to the orchestrator. Ingestion lands media in object storage (GCS/S3); transcription produces structured transcripts; LLM agents produce summaries, decisions, and action items written to Postgres. Phase 2 adds a vector store for retrieval/chat. Long-running steps run as async jobs with a queue.

  • Frontend React / Next.js, component library
  • Storage Object store (GCS/S3) for media + artifacts
  • Data PostgreSQL — users, projects, meetings, transcripts, topics
  • STT Speech-to-text + diarization service
  • LLM Claude / GPT for summary & extraction
  • Retrieval Vector DB (Phase 2) for RAG chat
  • Search Elasticsearch / Typesense (Phase 2)
  • Ops CI/CD, monitoring (Sentry / Datadog)

Consent & privacy model

Recording and processing meeting data is opt-in and explicit. The model:

  • One-time onboarding consent — the user agrees that Prism processes their meeting data to transcribe, generate notes, and generate insights.
  • Live-capture transparency — the bot is a visible attendee with join/leave states and permission prompts; participants can see it is present.
  • Scoped access — calendar and platform access granted via OAuth, limited to what each agent needs.
  • Data control — retention, deletion, and export are user-controllable; recordings are owned by the user/org.

Open privacy decisions

› Where is data stored (region / single-tenant vs multi-tenant)?
› Default retention period for recordings & transcripts?
› Which LLM provider, and do we need a no-training data agreement?

11 · How we scale to next-gen

The bet

Commodity summarizers stop at "here's a recap." Prism scales by treating each meeting as a node in an evolving project memory, by pairing Otter-style intelligence with a Loom-style shareable library so capture and async sharing compound, and by keeping the multi-agent core modular so new capability is a new agent — not a rewrite. Its real moat is the step past the summary: generating insights and tracking how features evolve to execution, so the app reports not just what was said but what shipped.

Three levers carry it from POC to next-gen: a stable output schema (so every agent and integration speaks the same language), a retrieval corpus that grows with every meeting, and execution sync that turns extracted insights and stories into real, tracked work in Jira and Git.

Scaling levers

  • Schema-first agents — typed contracts let us swap models/providers and add agents safely.
  • Growing memory — the vector corpus compounds; the more meetings, the better the insights.
  • Insight & drift over recap — generated insights and feature-drift tracking are the moat, not transcription.
  • Execution sync — Jira/Git export with two-way status closes the loop from talk to shipped.
  • Multi-platform parity — one capture pattern across audio, screen/video, links, and all four meeting platforms.
