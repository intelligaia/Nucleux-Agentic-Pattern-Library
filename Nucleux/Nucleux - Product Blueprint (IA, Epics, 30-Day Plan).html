Framing — Prism is one app with many cooperating agents

Prism is a whole package of one app — an intelligent meeting summarizer and insight generator — built from nine specialized agents working in combination (Orchestrator, Capture, Transcription, Summarizer, Insight, Action-item, Retrieval/Chat, Tracking/Drift, Integration). It is not a library of separate products. Prism captures meetings (record audio, capture screen/video, upload, paste a Drive/Zoom/Meet link, or import from Google Calendar), produces summaries, decisions, and action items, generates insights, and tracks how meeting insights and features evolve — detecting drift and exporting finalized user stories to Jira and Git with two-way status sync. This blueprint covers every page of that one app. “Prism” is a working name, subject to change.

Product direction. Prism blends Otter (live transcript, speaker ID, AI summary, keywords, action items, powerful search, calendar, ask-your-meetings) and Loom (recording library of video cards, async shareable playback, view tracking, timestamped comments/reactions) — then goes further with insight generation, feature/drift tracking, and execution sync to Jira/Git.

01 · Product-led growth strategy

The Prism PLG loop

Prism grows through the product, not through a sales motion. Anyone can sign up, upload or connect a meeting, and reach value in minutes. Value pulls in teammates; shared recordings (Loom-style) carry the app to new colleagues; more meetings make the intelligence — search, chat, insights, feature tracking — sharply better. The loop compounds, and once features start shipping to Jira/Git the app becomes part of the delivery workflow, not a side tool.

The aha-moment

Activation is not "watched a transcript scroll by." It is the first actionable summary — a user reads a clean summary card and sees correctly-extracted decisions and action items with owners, recognizes them as real, and does something with them (edits, exports, assigns, or shares). That is the instant the Meeting Summarizer earns the right to keep their meetings.

Everything in onboarding is engineered to reach this moment with the least possible friction: upload path first (no integration wait), a sample meeting available for instant gratification, and an activation checklist on the Day-0 dashboard that makes the next best action obvious.

Activation metric

  • Primary (individual) — first actionable summary reviewed within the first session.
  • Primary (team) — ≥3 members active and ≥1 shared project within 14 days.
  • Leading signals — time-to-first-summary, action-items accepted/edited, second meeting ingested, first search or chat query.

Expansion paths

  • Team adoption — invites and shared projects move a single user to a workspace; seats and meeting volume drive the plan.
  • Agents platform — enabling more agents (Insight, Retrieval/Chat, Integration) deepens value per seat and is the natural up-sell surface.
  • Live capture — once a team connects calendars and the bot starts joining automatically, ingestion volume — and stickiness — steps up.
  • Integrations — pushing action items to Jira makes Nucleux part of the delivery workflow, not a side tool.

PLG nudges baked into the UI

› Day-0 activation checklist on the dashboard home.
› "Invite the people who were in this meeting" after a summary.
› Empty-state prompts that demo search & chat with the user's own data.
› Weekly insight digest that pulls users back in.

N0

Nucleux Library Home & App Switcher

P1

Onboarding

Epics: E1 Onboarding & Activation · E2 Consent & Integrations · E3 Ingestion · Phase: POC

Purpose. Carry a new user from account to first value in four steps — workspace → connect platforms → one-time consent → first capture — minimizing friction at every gate. The upload path is offered first so value never waits on integration.

FeaturesStep 1 create workspace (name, optional team domain) · Step 2 connect meeting platforms via OAuth (Meet/Zoom/Teams/WebEx, calendar) · Step 3 one-time consent screen · Step 4 first capture (upload a file or schedule the bot) · progress rail · "skip for now" on connect/schedule.

As a new user, I want to create a workspace in one screen, so that I can start without configuring anything.

Acceptance criteria

  • Workspace requires only a name; a sensible default is pre-filled from the user's email domain.
  • Team/domain capture is optional and clearly skippable.

As a new user, I want to skip connecting a platform and just upload a recording, so that I get a summary today.

Acceptance criteria

  • The connect step has a prominent "skip for now — I'll upload" path.
  • Skipping connect still completes onboarding and lands the user on the Day-0 dashboard.
  • The upload step accepts desktop file, Google Drive, and a shared link.

As a new user, I want to grant one clear consent rather than repeated prompts, so that I understand and trust what the Meeting Summarizer processes.

Acceptance criteria

  • A single consent screen explains what is processed, why, and how to revoke; consent is recorded with timestamp and version.
  • Consent is required before any ingest; it is captured once, not per meeting.

As a workspace owner, I want to connect my calendar and a meeting platform during onboarding, so that future meetings are captured automatically.

Acceptance criteria

  • OAuth flows for calendar and each platform are scoped to the minimum required permissions.
  • Connected state is reflected immediately and the Scheduler agent begins watching the calendar.
  • A failed/declined OAuth returns the user to the step with a retry, not a dead end.

05 · Onboarding & consent flow

P1, step by step

The wizard is engineered for the shortest credible path to the aha-moment. The upload path is always available so a user can reach a first summary even if they connect nothing.

STEP 1

Create workspace

Name only (pre-filled from email domain). Optional team domain. One screen.

STEP 2

Connect platforms

OAuth to calendar + Meet/Zoom/Teams/WebEx. Scoped permissions. Skippable.

STEP 3

One-time consent

Single, explicit consent to process meeting data. Recorded with version + timestamp.

STEP 4

First ingest

Upload a file/Drive/link, or schedule the bot for an upcoming meeting.

DONE

Land on Day-0

Dashboard with the activation checklist; first summary processing.

The one-time consent screen — requirements

  • Plain-language scope — states that Nucleux processes meeting audio/video and transcripts to generate summaries, decisions, and action items.
  • Why & who — names the processing purpose and that recordings are owned by the user/org.
  • Live-capture transparency — explains the bot joins as a visible attendee with join/leave states and permission prompts.
  • Control — links to retention, export, and deletion controls and how to revoke (P7 Settings).
  • Record of consent — captured once, stored with user, timestamp, and consent version; required before any ingest.

Suggested copy: “Nucleux will process the meetings you upload or connect — including audio, video, and transcripts — to generate summaries, decisions, and action items. Your recordings belong to you and your organization. You can review, export, or delete them, and revoke access, anytime in Settings.”

OAuth connection — requirements

  • Least privilege — request only the calendar/platform scopes each agent needs (read events; join meetings the user owns/attends).
  • Per-platform — Meet, Zoom, Teams, WebEx, and calendar are independent connections; any subset can be granted.
  • Graceful skip — declining or skipping returns to the wizard with the upload path intact; never a dead end.
  • Immediate effect — a successful connection starts the Scheduler agent watching that calendar.

Agents engaged during onboarding

› Scheduler/Calendar — on calendar connect (Step 2).
› Capture — when a live meeting is scheduled (Step 4).
› Orchestrator → Transcription → Summarizer → Action-item — on first ingest.

07 · Analytics we unlock later

What the corpus makes possible

Once a team adopts and meetings accumulate, two analytics surfaces open up: team product analytics (for owners and leads, on P6 Analytics & Insights, richest at Day 30) and the user-facing Insight agent outputs (woven into the Library, recordings, and the weekly digest). Both are powered by the Insight agent over the project corpus.

Charts & signals

  • Topic-frequency trend — a line/area chart of how often each topic appears across a project's meetings over time; reveals what the team keeps circling.
  • Decision-change diff timeline — a timeline marking where a prior decision was changed or reversed, each node opening a side-by-side before/after diff with links to both meetings.
  • Progress-vs-repetition gauge — a gauge contrasting new ground covered against repeated discussion, so a lead can see if meetings are advancing or stalling.
  • Meeting-health score — a composite score per project (decisions made, action follow-through, repetition, attendance) with an explanation of what's dragging it down.
  • Adoption funnel — invited → signed-up → first-summary → habitual, the product-analytics view of how a team is taking up Nucleux.

Team product analytics (for the org)

  • Meeting load — volume and time spent in meetings per team/project.
  • Decision velocity — decisions made per period, and how many later changed.
  • Recurring-topic trends — which themes dominate and whether they're resolving.
  • Meeting health — aggregate hygiene across projects.
  • Adoption funnel — activation and habit across members.

User-facing Insight outputs

› "Here's what changed since your last meeting on X."
› "This topic has come up 5 times with no decision."
› Weekly digest: decisions made, items still open, stale threads.
