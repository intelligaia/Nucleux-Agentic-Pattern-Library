Naming + mark studies.

Six product-name candidates — each paired with a custom glyph that fits the name's specific meaning rather than re-using the cube mark. The first option, Intelli Agent, is the most brand-DNA fit — half of the parent name carried directly into the product. The next four are progressively more independent.

Intelli Agent 01 / 06
Coined · Intelli(gaia) + Agent
Most brand-DNA option. Half of the parent name ("Intelli") fused with the literal product subject ("Agent"). Reads as a phrase, not a coined word — meaning is immediate, no explanation needed. Glyph: a lowercase i whose dot is replaced with a four-ray sparkle. Intelligaia's "i" carrying the AI activity indicator. Quiet, intelligent, subtle.

How each name fits

Intelli Agent — strongest brand-DNA name. Half of "Intelligaia" carried directly into the product name. Zero explanation needed: the name is the description. Best fit if you want the product to read as a first-party Intelligaia offering, not a separately-branded sub-brand.

Praxia — strongest positioning name. Built into the word is the differentiator (applied vs theoretical). Coined, fully ownable trademark. Best fit if you want the library to read as a working method, not a reference book.

Cairn — strongest metaphor name. Stones marking a trail = patterns marking the AI journey. Earthy, sturdy, design-trade flavour, unclaimed.

Mira — strongest concept name. "Look." Three letters, two syllables, lands instantly. Slight risk: Mira Studios (Hollywood) and a few smaller products use it; brand presence is diluted.

Folio — strongest editorial name. Publication / craft tone, signals the library reads like a magazine. Slight risk: Folio.io is a finance tool — different category but the .com is taken.

Lumen — strongest tech name. SI unit gives it precision. Slight risk: Lumen Technologies (formerly CenturyLink) is huge; brand collision in B2B.

My pick from this set: Intelli Agent. It says exactly what it is, carries the parent brand, doesn't require coining or explaining a metaphor, and the sparkle-dotted "i" glyph is small enough to use anywhere. Praxia and Cairn remain strong fallbacks if you want more independent identity.
